Plain-language explainer

How the platform canopy works

The idea in one sentence: a flat platform goes under the bed, its three curved half-moon sides slide out until they clear the bed, and a stroller-style hood swings up from the back to cover the bed. It's closed on the left, right, and back, and open at the front. The bed and your dog sit on the platform, so their weight holds everything in place. Nothing clamps or straps to the bed itself.

  1. Slide under. Lift the front of the bed an inch or two and push the folded platform underneath, with the grippy side up.
  2. Size it. Pull out the left, right, and back half-moon wings until each curved edge sticks out about 2 inches past the bed. They click as they go.
  3. Pull the canopy over. Lay the folded canopy flat around the back and sides and snap its two hubs onto the front tips of the side wings. Then grab the front hoop's handle and swing it up and over until it clicks upright at the front.
  4. Done. Your dog walks in through the open front.

1 · How the half-moon sides adjust

The center plate stays under the bed. Each of the three half-moon wings rides on two telescoping rails (tube inside tube) that run into the center plate. A spring pawl on each rail clicks over ratchet teeth every ½ inch, so a wing slides out easily but can't creep back in. To pull a wing in, press its thumb lever. Each side sets on its own, so an off-center bed still gets even clearance. The rails give 6 to 10 inches of side travel, enough for the narrow, standard, and wide beds shown above.

2 · How the canopy hinges and locks

The canopy works like a stroller hood. It has five hoops that share one pivot hub on each side, that snaps into a socket at the front tip of the left and right wings once the sides are sized. The hubs can stay snapped on between uses. The bottom hoop stays down and sits in a Velcro channel along the wing rims. The other four fold flat on top of it, around the back and bottom of the bed. When you pull the front hoop up and over, the fabric lifts each hoop in turn. Once the front hoop is upright, a spring pin in each hub drops into a notch and locks it. To fold it, squeeze both hub buttons. Each hoop has a telescoping sleeve at the top center, so it widens and narrows as the hubs move out with the wings.

3 · Why it stays put

The center plate carries the full weight of the bed and the dog, and its grip top and silicone feet keep it from sliding. Each wing is held to that plate by two rails, so the canopy's load runs from the hubs to the wings, through the rails, and into the weighted plate. The whole platform is under half an inch tall and lies flat on the floor, so it has no tipping point. The canopy is light, about 1½ lb, and its weight sits inside the footprint. Leaning on it or bumping it pushes against the bed's weight, not a clamp.

4 · Sizing the front opening

The front hoop is the doorway. Its width follows the hub spacing, so it widens automatically when you widen the sides. Its height is about 25 inches on all three sample beds, which lets a small-to-medium dog walk in without ducking. There is no crossbar on the floor, only the half-inch wing tips, so nothing trips your dog at the entrance. The hubs sit about 2 to 4 inches in front of the bed, so the hoop never presses on the front bolster.

5 · Washing it

The fabric is one piece. Its bottom hem has hook tape that presses onto the loop tape on the wing rims, and soft Velcro tabs wrap around each hoop. To wash it, fold the canopy down, peel the tabs and the hem, and lift the fabric off. The hoops and hubs stay snapped on the platform. Machine wash the fabric cold on gentle and air dry. Wipe or rinse the platform. Press the fabric back on the same way.

6 · Why closed on three sides

A curled-up dog gives off heat, and warm air rises and drifts. With the left, right, and back closed down to the platform, that warm air pools under the hood instead of escaping. The open front lets your dog get in and out, gives them a view, and lets stale air out.

The core innovation

One canopy, five attachment modes

Start with Mode E, the slide-under platform. It is the most universal mode. It works on any bed with a flat underside and needs no rim thickness, lip, or surface texture. Its left, right, and back half-moon sides slide out and click until they clear the bed, so the canopy footprint follows the bed's size. The dog's own weight is the fastener. Its stroller-style canopy pulls up from the back and over the top, closing the left, right, and back and leaving only the front open.

For travel, crates, or beds that get dragged around, use Modes A–D. Two included Rim Strips stay on the bed. Their underside ports take any of four tool-free hardware types with a quarter-turn bayonet, so you choose whatever fits your bed and can mix modes on the same bed. The canopy then presses onto the Rim Strips' Velcro loop face.

Canopy construction

A three-panel arc that fits round and rectangular beds

Two side wings join a center dome through a row of hinge knuckles along each seam. The hinge moves from a tight 120° curl for round and donut beds to a nearly flat 175° span for rectangular beds and crate pads.

  • 8 knuckles per seam, 0.375 × 0.75 in, spaced 2.0 in apart
  • 0.125 in / 3.18 mm stainless pin with friction washers
  • 1 in center fold gap, so the unit ships flat in the FBA small standard tier
  • Sizes S / M / L fit beds up to 24×18, 32×24, and 42×32 in

Shape memory

Wire ribs hold any angle you set

Each panel has internal channels with bendable 2.0 mm annealed steel ribs inside them. Bend the canopy once and it keeps that angle without sagging onto your dog.

  • 12 ribs total: 4 per wing at 11 in, 4 in the dome at 13 in
  • 4.0 mm channel ID · wings spaced 4.0 in / 101.6 mm apart
  • Paw-safe ends: 6 mm silicone caps, bar-tacked channel ends, 0.5 in fabric overrun
  • Fabric stack: 300D recycled oxford shell with DWR, 100 g/m² hollow-fiber batting, an optional perforated reflective scrim, and a minky lining

Velcro interface

Peel it off, wash it, and press it back on

The canopy edge carries a 1.5 in / 38 mm hook strip. The Rim Strips on the bed carry the matching loop strip. Clamps, straps, flaps, and discs stay installed while the canopy goes in the wash.

  • Hook run is the wing width minus 1 in (17 in on Medium). The loop runs 1 in longer.
  • Rated for 5,000+ peel cycles
  • Fold the fabric guard flap over the hook, then wash cold on gentle in a laundry bag with no softener. Tumble dry low or line dry. Ribs and knuckles stay in.

Mode decision guide

Which attachment mode fits your bed?

Start with the floor. If the bed has a flat underside and stays put indoors, slide the platform under it (Mode E). For travel or crates, check the rim. If it's rigid, clamp it. If it's soft, strap it. If it has a lip, tuck it. If it's slick plastic, use suction. Modes can be mixed per corner.

Differentiation

Every competitor sells a whole bed. ThermoCanopy upgrades the bed you already have.

BarkBox/BARK hideaway beds, PetFusion covered beds, and the crowded Amazon field of cave, igloo, and tent beds all bundle the cover with the bed. These are the review themes we heard, paraphrased.

Gap 1 · Overheating

"The closed hood traps too much heat, and my dog pants and leaves the bed."

The front stays open, the wing angle meters the warmth, and the $15 mesh swap gives shade with airflow in summer.

Gap 2 · Collapsing hoods

"The hood sags flat onto my dog after a few weeks."

Wire ribs and friction hinge knuckles give the canopy a mechanical structure that stays where you set it.

Gap 3 · Can't wash it

"The cover is sewn around the frame, so I can only spot-clean it."

The canopy peels off the Velcro in one motion and goes in the washer whole, while the hardware stays on the bed.

Gap 4 · Not universal

"My dog loves his bed, and I don't want to replace it to get a cover."

Five attachment modes, led by a slide-under platform that fits any flat-bottomed bed, plus a flexing arc and three sizes, fit round, bolster, orthopedic, and crate beds.

Privacy screen for anxious dogs. Mount one wing plus the dome toward the busiest side of the room, or clamp it to crate bars as a partial cover. The dog gets a den with a view of the exit, which is safer than a draped blanket and less confining than a full cave.
