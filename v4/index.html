V4 · Manufacturer build document · Revision D

OverCover V4

Fixed-size three-panel detachable canopy system, built in three sizes. It has a three-panel house-profile arc, spring-steel ribs, and a flat base panel with peel-and-stick hook strips.

  • ★ Three fixed sizes S / M / L (no telescoping rib)
  • Two side wings + center dome, hinge knuckles
  • 0.250 in A229 / A228 spring-steel ribs
  • Flat base, hook-side-up adhesive strips
  • Hard floor: 20.0 in center clearance
  • Summer mesh panel: separate item

What you are building. Each SKU has a flat base panel with high-tack acrylic adhesive hook strips (hook up) around its perimeter. A canopy cover is built from a left wing panel, a center dome panel and a right wing panel, joined by two rows of hinge knuckles. Formed 0.250 in spring-steel ribs in lined channels hold the house profile. The canopy's bottom-edge loop tape presses onto the base hook strips, so the cover can be removed for washing. Rib length, panel widths and base span are fixed per size. Nothing adjusts in the field.

System overview · what ships per SKU

Three subassemblies per size, one separate mesh item

Each OverCover V4 SKU (Small, Medium, Large) ships with three subassemblies built for that size only. Parts are not interchangeable across sizes: the rib form, the panel pattern and the base strip layout are all sized to one fixed base span S. V4 has no telescoping rib, slide-under platform, ratchet rail or hub. The Large SKU is the reference geometry: a 68 in arc (18 in center + 2 × 25 in wings) over a 48 in span. Dimensions are inches, with millimetres where useful (1 in = 25.4 mm exactly).

SUBASSEMBLY A

Flat base panel

A 6 mm corona-treated PP twin-wall panel (alternate: 0.125 in ABS) with sealed edges and anti-slip underside dots. High-tack acrylic adhesive hook strips are applied hook side up around the perimeter, with at least 2 strips per side.

SUBASSEMBLY B

Formed rib set

0.250 in oil-tempered spring steel (ASTM A229; ASTM A228 music wire allowed), CNC-formed to the SKU's house profile with two R 1.5 in knuckle bends. Nylon-11 coated, with end caps retained by dual O-rings. 3 / 4 / 5 ribs for S / M / L.

SUBASSEMBLY C

Three-panel canopy cover

Left wing, center dome and right wing panels, joined by two rows of hinge knuckles, plus a back gable panel. Rib channels have UHMW liners and removal plackets. A ridge tape runs across the center panel. The bottom-edge flange carries sewn loop tape.

SEPARATE ITEM

Summer mesh panel

A complete three-panel cover plus gable in 3D spacer mesh, one per size. It uses the same pattern, knuckles, lined channels and loop flange. The ribs transfer from the standard cover, so the mesh item ships without ribs.

Three-panel geometry · house / barn profile · inverse arc

House-profile geometry and the 20.0 in clearance floor

The canopy profile is a house shape. Two straight wing panels of length Wg rise from the rib feet at the base edges (span S) to two hinge-knuckle lines a chord C apart, and the center dome panel spans the knuckles. Treating the knuckles as sharp corners and the center as a flat chord, the rib-centerline height at the center is:

h = √( Wg² − ((S − C) / 2)² )

Inverse arc: span is the enemy of height

For a fixed wing length, a wider span tilts each wing further from vertical, so the knuckle line drops. The relationship is the same inverse arc used in V3: dh/dS = −(S − C) / (4h). That is −0.375 in per inch of span on the Large SKU. Holding Wg = 25 in and C = 18 in and sweeping the span:

  • S = 48 in → h = 20.0 in (the hard floor)
  • S = 36 in → h ≈ 23.3 in
  • S = 24 in → h ≈ 24.8 in

V4 removes the adjustable span. Each SKU's rib is formed for exactly one S, so h is fixed at manufacture. The floor is therefore checked at each size's own span, which is also that size's largest span.

Credited vs uncredited height. The formula gives the rib-centerline height at the knuckle line. The center rib segment is formed with a 0.50 in crown, and rib radius + liner + lining remove about 0.25 in. That leaves an inner-face center clearance of h + 0.25 in. QC passes on the inner face ≥ 20.0 in.

Engineering clearance requirement

Every SKU must hold an inner-face center clearance of ≥ 20.0 in across the full center chord C and the full rib-station depth. The Large SKU is at the floor by design, so its geometry tolerances are one-sided (see tolerances).

Why not one 68 in kit on all spans

On a 24 in span, a 25 in wing stands at 83° (nearly vertical), with a 97° knuckle and 24.8 in of height. That uses excess fabric and steel, makes a tall, narrow shape that racks easily, and leaves little clear width near the base plane. The per-SKU rule keeps all three sizes within 20.1–20.5 in of rib height.

Why not proportional scaling

Scaling the whole Large profile by 0.75 (Medium) or 0.50 (Small) also scales h: 15.0 in and 10.0 in. Both fail the 20.0 in floor. So the center chord and wings are resized per SKU rather than scaled.

Canopy-to-base interface · peel-and-stick hook strips

Flat base panel: hook side up, loop on the canopy hem

The base panel is a flat sheet. The load rests on its top face; the load is not supplied. Peel-and-stick hook strips with a high-tack acrylic adhesive go around the perimeter of the top face, hook side up. The canopy's bottom edge is a 2.0 in inward-turned flange carrying sewn loop tape. That flange lies flat on the strips, so the cover detaches for washing and the base stays put. V3's slide-under platform, half-moon wings, ratchet rails and hubs are all deleted.

Strip layout rules

  • Hook side up, 1.5 in / 38 mm wide nylon hook with high-tack acrylic PSA (low-surface-energy grade for PP). Outer edge inset 0.25 in from the panel edge.
  • At least 2 strips per side. Strips are segmented with equal gaps ≥ 1.0 in and 2.0 in corner setbacks (measured from the perpendicular edge, which clears the adjoining strip band), so a peel front stops at a gap instead of running the whole side.
  • Die-cut strip corners at R 0.25 in. Square corners start peels.
  • Load path in shear, not peel. The loop flange lies flat on the hook, parallel to the base. Ribs are formed 0.50 in wider than S, so each rib foot pushes outward in the plane of the strip (shear). Canopy weight presses the flange down.
  • The front entry zone between the corner strips is left bare. There is no exposed hook in the entry traffic path. When the cover is off, loop guard covers (A6) go over all hook.
  • Loop tape on the canopy is sewn, never adhesive, so it survives washing.

Frame · ribs, channels, knuckles

Formed spring-steel ribs through lined panel channels

Each rib station is one continuous 0.250 in oil-tempered spring-steel rib (ASTM A229; ASTM A228 music wire is an allowed equal). It is CNC-formed to the SKU's house profile: a straight wing leg, an R 1.5 in bend at the knuckle line, a center segment with a 0.50 in crown, a second R 1.5 in bend, and a second wing leg. The rib runs inside a sewn channel across all three panels. The channel crosses each knuckle row through a gap in the knuckles.

Steel rib (standard)

A229 or A228, Ø 0.250 in ±0.002. Cut to the developed length. Ends chamfered 0.03 in × 45° and deburred. Two bends, inside radius ≥ 1.5 in (6 × d; no cracking or surface checking). Stress-relieve after forming per the wire supplier's schedule (typically 450 °F / 232 °C, 30 min). Zinc-phosphate pretreat, then nylon-11 powder coat 0.010–0.015 in. The free span is formed to S + 0.50 in to give an outward shear preload.

Fiberglass rib (alternate)

Pultruded fiberglass rod Ø 0.312 in. FRP cannot be bent to R 1.5 in: at an allowable strain of about 1.5%, its minimum elastic radius is about 10 in. So the FRP option uses three straight segments per station (two wings + one center) joined at each knuckle by a molded PA66-GF30 fixed-angle elbow set to that SKU's knuckle angle. The internal guide radius stays ≥ 1.5 in. Rod ends seat in the elbow sockets and are retained by dual O-rings. Segment ends are capped and sleeved against fiber bloom.

Hinge knuckles

Molded PA6-GF15 knuckles (OD 0.375 × 0.75 in long) on 0.75 in webbing tabs, with alternating panel ownership, on a Ø 0.125 in 304 stainless pin. They run in groups of 3 between rib stations at 2.0 in on center, with one pin per group. The knuckles locate the seam line and let the cover fold flat when the ribs are out. In use, the formed rib sets the knuckle angle (106.6° / 117.2° / 126.9° for S / M / L), so the knuckles carry no structural moment.

Lined channels and removal placket

Each channel is a 1.0 in flat sleeve with a 0.010 in UHMW-PE tape liner (alternate: woven nylon 6,6 liner) on the outer-face bearing side, full length. The ribs come out for washing through an inner-face 1.0 in hook-and-loop placket running the full channel length. A formed rib cannot be pulled lengthwise through its own bends.

Rib feet and keeper O-rings

Each rib end carries a molded PA66 foot cap (OD 0.50 in, 1.25 in long, 0.50 in seat depth). Two internal glands hold dual O-rings, AS568-010 (0.239 ID × 0.070 CS): Buna-N 70A for the indoor standard, EPDM 70A for the outdoor variant. Assemble with silicone grease only, never petroleum products on Buna. User-replaceable spares ship in the kit. The cap sits in a reinforced foot pocket at the hem.

Ridge tape across center panel

1.0 in PP webbing runs on the center-panel centerline from the front rib station to the back gable. It is bar-tacked to every rib channel at the exact station pitch (±0.06 in). It keeps the stations parallel and spaced, stops mid-sag of the center panel between ribs, and resists racking. It shares mid-span load between ribs, so no single rib is pushed into buckling.

Factory build sequence

How to build it

Build A, B and C in parallel and join them at final assembly. Every kit is checked on its own size's QC base before pack-out. Do not mix parts across sizes. Each part is marked S, M or L at the point it is made.

  1. A · Base panel

    1. Cut or rout the panel (A1) to S × D, +0 / −0.12 in, with R 1.0 in corners. Seal the edges (A2).
    2. Dyne-pen check the top skin: ≥ 42 dyn/cm (PP). Re-treat or reject below that.
    3. Wipe the strip footprints with 70% IPA and let them flash. Apply adhesion promoter (A5) to the footprints only (PP).
    4. Place the hook strips (A4) hook side up in a locating jig: 0.25 in edge inset, equal gaps ≥ 1.0 in, 2.0 in corner setbacks, per the strip schedule. Roll down at about 15 psi, 2 passes.
    5. Fit the anti-slip dots (A3) on the underside on a 6 in grid.
    6. Dwell 24 h minimum (72 h for QC samples) at ≥ 65 °F. Fit the guard covers (A6) for shipping.

  2. B · Rib set

    1. Cut A229/A228 0.250 in wire to the cut length (52.43 / 59.67 / 66.82 in ±0.06). Chamfer and deburr both ends.
    2. CNC-bend: bend tangent points at 19.38 / 21.58 / 23.75 in from each end, inside radius 1.5 in. Form the 0.50 in center crown. Compensate for springback so the free span reads S + 0.50 in.
    3. Stress-relieve per the wire supplier's schedule (typically 450 °F / 232 °C, 30 min). Air cool.
    4. Zinc-phosphate pretreat, then nylon-11 powder coat at 0.010–0.015 in. Holiday-check sample ribs.
    5. Gauge every rib on the SKU profile fixture: free span, height, bend radius gauge, and planarity ≤ 0.12 in.
    6. Lightly coat the O-rings (B3) with silicone grease only, seat two per cap (B2), and press the caps onto the rib ends to the 0.50 in seat.

  3. C · Canopy cover

    1. Cut the shell, batting and lining for the left wing, center dome, right wing and back gable to the SKU pattern. Quilt on a 2.0 in diamond grid, keeping the channel lanes clear.
    2. Sew the rib channel sleeves (C4) at each station position (1.0 in inset from front and back, then equal pitch). Stitch the UHMW liner (C5) on the outer-face side. Sew the full-length placket (C6) on the inner face.
    3. Sew the knuckle tabs (C7) into the two wing-to-dome seams in groups of 3 between stations, alternating panel ownership. Leave a 1.5 in channel gap at each station. Thread the pins (C8) and peen the ends.
    4. Sew the back gable to the three panels. Turn the left, right and back hems inward into the 2.0 in flange and sew the loop tape (C10) to it. Add the front corner tabs and pull tabs (C13).
    5. Sew the foot pockets (C11) at each rib foot. Bar-tack the ridge tape (C9) on the center-panel centerline at each station, at the exact pitch.
    6. Bind all edges (C12). Trim threads. Check that no needle holes are left in the liner bearing lane.

  4. D · Final assembly, test and pack-out

    1. Insert each capped rib into its channel through the placket. Seat the feet in the foot pockets and close the placket.
    2. Place the canopy on the size's QC base. Press the loop flange onto the hook strips with a hand roller.
    3. Measure the inner-face center clearance at every rib station with a height gauge. It must be ≥ 20.0 in (nominal 20.37 / 20.71 / 20.25 in). Reject anything below 20.0 in.
    4. Visual check: knuckle rows straight, ridge tape taut, no rib cap exposed, no hook exposed outside the flange.
    5. Remove the ribs. Nest the ribs (same profile stacks). Fold the cover at the knuckle seams. Pack the base flat with guard covers on, the cover, the nested ribs and the spares pack (B4) in one carton.

Separate item · summer mesh panel

Summer mesh panel swap

The summer mesh panel is built, labeled and packed as its own item, one per size (S / M / L). It is a complete three-panel cover plus back gable in 3D spacer mesh. It uses the same pattern, hinge knuckles, lined rib channels, ridge tape and loop flange as the standard cover. To swap, peel the standard cover off the hook strips, move the ribs through the plackets, and press the mesh cover on. The base is not touched.

  • 3D spacer mesh, 2.0 mm, over an open-knit liner. No batting and no fleece.
  • The channel liner (UHMW) and plackets are identical, so the ribs transfer one-for-one.
  • The mesh item must pass the same clearance, wash and channel-wear QC.

Changes vs V3 (Rev C → Rev D): The slide-under platform, half-moon wings, telescoping ratchet rails and hinge hubs are deleted. So is the five-hoop frame: 8.5 mm fiberglass hoops, the rear hoops' shock-corded slip joints (the telescoping rib), and the 2.0 mm wire shaping ribs. There are three fixed sizes, each with its own formed rib set in 0.250 in A229/A228 spring steel (FRP 0.312 in segmented alternate). The attachment is now a flat base panel with adhesive hook strips (hook up) and a sewn loop flange. A ridge tape runs across the center panel. Lined channels with removal plackets and keeper O-ring foot caps are added. The variable-span inverse arc is replaced by a fixed-span house profile with a 20.0 in clearance floor checked per size.
